Calculator layout, round 23

The detailed demo reads better than the live calculator, but it takes more clicks and spreads one fighter over separate pages. These mocks keep the demo's look and bring back the live app's strength: everything on one screen, changing as you build. The numbers are Goku (Z - Early) and Janemba from the demo. Click the mocks: capsules, More, Quick / Full and Find all work.

1. One fighter page, the build beside it Sheet + Full stats + capsule picker

Sheet and Full stats become one page with one section order. Each section shows its quick numbers; More opens the full rows right there. The build stays in the left rail, and the capsule picker opens inside that rail, so the numbers stay in view while you swap capsules.

Trade-off: the rail gives up the big art while picking capsules (it comes back on Done), and the page is longer with everything on Full. Quick is the default.

  • 1Headline strip, pinned while scrolling: the numbers checked first, updating with every change.
  • 2Quick / Full opens every section at once; each section's More opens just that one.
  • 3Find a stat: type to jump to any stat or move, even inside closed sections.
  • 4Capsules open in the rail. Click to add or remove; hover to preview the change on the strip.
Phone: the same page; the build is a half-height sheet, with what changed pinned above it

2. One Versus page Compare + Vs opponent

Compare and Vs opponent become one page. A switch shows the numbers Side by side (each fighter as they are) or as the Matchup (each one's damage after the other's defense and armor). The rows stay where they are, so you see exactly which numbers the matchup changes. Each fighter's build sits on its own side, as in the live calculator's compare mode.

Trade-off: two rails take width, so the middle is narrower than today's Compare. On a tablet the rails fold into Loadout buttons, as the phone shows.

3. Quieter type easier to scan

With more data on the page, the numbers should do the talking. Labels go plain and small; only numbers are bold, with digits lined up. Slants stay on tabs and headers, not on every tile.

Trade-off: a little less of the game's look inside the sections. The headers, tabs, art and colours keep it.
